# Retroid Pocket Mini V2 heat: online and offline investigation (2026-09-24)

**Answer: there is a real cooling configuration gap, but the heat's full cause is unverified.** The read-only USB serial probe stopped at **95.4°C** (`cpu7-top-thermal`), after seeing **84.5°C** (`cpu0-thermal`) and **78.7°C** (`aoss0-thermal`). These are internal chip sensors, not case or battery measurements. The owner powered the device off. The image kernel omits the board's PWM fan driver and its PWM provider. Neither fan rotation nor effective CPU governor nor the active thermal trip policy was observed on the booted device. No further boot is requested here.

## Primary evidence

1. **Units and meaning (verified for the Linux ABI).** The kernel [thermal sysfs ABI](https://www.kernel.org/doc/Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-class-thermal) specifies `thermal_zoneX/temp` as the current zone temperature and `trip_point_Y_temp` in **millidegree Celsius**; `type` identifies the zone and `trip_point_Y_type` identifies the trip (for example `passive` or `critical`). The [kernel thermal sysfs guide](https://docs.kernel.org/driver-api/thermal/sysfs-api.html) lists `temp`, `type`, `policy`, `mode`, trip temperatures/types/hysteresis, and cooling-device interfaces. Zone labels are not surface-temperature measurements. The [thermal-zone DT binding](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/Documentation/devicetree/bindings/thermal/thermal-zones.yaml) defines the sensor reference, trip, hysteresis, and cooling map as *separate* properties: a temperature does not itself identify what cooling actually happened.

2. **SM8250 sensor and policy (verified source, not yet verified on the running DTB).** The exact upstream [Linux 7.2 `sm8250.dtsi`](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.2/arch/arm64/boot/dts/qcom/sm8250.dtsi) names `cpu7-top-thermal`, assigns `<&tsens0 10>`, and gives it 90°C and 95°C passive trips plus a 110°C critical trip. It maps passive trips to CPU cooling devices. Its `aoss0-thermal` uses `<&tsens0 0>`. These names locate **TSENS channels**, not a physical thermocouple position or the outside-case temperature. The [historical patch that introduced those zones](https://patchwork.kernel.org/project/linux-pm/patch/bf5ca7777fbb6f5e2d374a9a72d1e17d485bd8ea.1591684754.git.amit.kucheria@linaro.org/) is background, not the deployed artifact. Kernel source alone does not prove actual throttling or the running DTB's policy.

3. **Device-specific provenance and missing normal baseline.** [Retroid's U-Boot repository](https://github.com/RetroidPocket/u-boot) describes a `sm8250-retroidpocket-rpminiv2` device tree used to build its **bootloader**; the [ROCKNIX Mini device page](https://rocknix.org/devices/retroid/retroid-pocket-mini/) identifies the Mini V2 selection. Neither bootloader build instructions nor that device page establishes which **Linux** DTB, overrides, governor, or cooling map Korri Linux 7.2 actually boots. No first-party Retroid/ROCKNIX Mini V2 Linux thermal DTS with a validated *idle normal temperature range* was established in this online pass. **There is no supported numerical idle-normal range here.** A trip threshold is a control threshold, not an expected idle temperature. There is no case/battery thermometry or sustained baseline in the given reading.

4. **Load evidence is limited (verified kernel definition; interpretation inferred).** The [kernel PSI guide](https://docs.kernel.org/accounting/psi.html) defines CPU `some avg10` as recent time with tasks stalled on CPU availability. `0.00` does **not** measure CPU watts, frequencies, GPU activity, sensor accuracy, or prior heat soak. A single `/proc/loadavg` value of `1.03` cannot distinguish those causes. Do not infer that the processor was thermally idle.

## Offline findings from the exact image source

The installed spare card was written from the `c36c76f3` image. `git show c36c76f3:nix/devices/rpminiv2/default.nix` selects `kernel/config-korri` for the product kernel. The exact commit's `config-korri` has `# CONFIG_HWMON is not set` (line 2259), and no `CONFIG_LEDS_QCOM_LPG`. The board's [copied DTS](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/dts/sm8250-retroidpocket-common.dtsi) declares `pwm-fan`, wired through `pm8150l_lpg` and `vreg_fan_pwr` (lines 41–49, 207–220, 1109–1111). Linux 7.2 [HWMON Kconfig](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.2/drivers/hwmon/Kconfig) gates `SENSORS_PWM_FAN` under `HWMON`, while [Qualcomm LPG Kconfig](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.2/drivers/leds/rgb/Kconfig) says `LEDS_QCOM_LPG` exposes PWM channels. The full diagnostic-only [`kernel/config`](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/config) retains `CONFIG_HWMON=y`, `CONFIG_SENSORS_PWM_FAN=m`, and `CONFIG_LEDS_QCOM_LPG=y` (lines 3511, 3641, 5815); the product does not use that config. **Verified from the configured source:** the product kernel cannot bind this DTS PWM fan through those Linux drivers. **Unverified on hardware:** whether another mechanism runs the fan or how much the missing driver contributes to the heat. The [fan boot-speed patch](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/patches/9997-set-boot-fanspeed.patch) sets PWM 70 inside the absent driver, so it cannot compensate.

The same exact product config selects `CONFIG_CPU_FREQ_DEFAULT_GOV_PERFORMANCE=y` (line 637), builds `schedutil` (line 648), and omits `CONFIG_QCOM_LMH` (line 2286). The [kernel CPUFreq guide](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/pm/cpufreq.html) says `performance` requests the maximum allowed frequency; user space can change the governor after boot. The full diagnostic config also defaults to performance, so this setting alone does not explain a change from ROCKNIX. Effective CPU frequency, governor, process use, GPU load, and charging were not sampled because the probe stopped at 95.4°C.

The [GPU tuning patch](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/patches/9998-gpu-tuning.patch) adds GPU steps up to 925 MHz and explicitly warns that its higher idle memory bandwidth vote can increase battery use. This patch is selected by [`kernel/default.nix`](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/default.nix). It is a candidate contributor, **not an observed cause**. The [OLED idle service](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/portal.nix) only powers off the Sway display after 300 seconds (lines 14–18). The [SD image definition](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/sd-image.nix) states that OLED blanking is not sleep (line 20), so a dark display does not prove low-power idle.

The exact upstream [Linux 7.2 SM8250 DTS](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.2/arch/arm64/boot/dts/qcom/sm8250.dtsi) lists two `cpu7-top-thermal` passive trips at **90°C** and **95°C**, with a critical trip at **110°C** (`cpu7-top-thermal`, `thermal-sensors = <&tsens0 10>`). None of the [local kernel patches](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/patches/) names that zone or its trips. **Inferred, pending inspection of the running DTB/sysfs:** 95.4°C crossed both source passive trips, not the critical trip. The critical value is not an acceptable operating target. The [copied board DTS](../../nix/devices/rpminiv2/kernel/dts/sm8250-retroidpocket-common.dtsi) defines distinct skin and PCB zones with 50°C passive trips (lines 76–99); neither was measured. A CPU hot-spot reading cannot be used as a skin reading.

The initial probe read only the first three thermal zones. It stopped before `ps`, fan/cooling state, CPU clocks, or battery state. It reported `loadavg 1.03 1.07 1.07` and CPU `some avg10=0.00`; neither proves an idle CPU. Its clock said `2024-12-19T22:20:15Z`, while the host date was `2026-09-24`, so compare future logs with boot-relative time. The earlier conversational **35–60°C** idle range was a generic guess, not an established Mini V2 range. Do not treat it as a baseline.

## Safest discriminating read-only checks, only if the owner later chooses to boot

| Priority | Short check | What it distinguishes; limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record booted `uname -r`, `/proc/device-tree/model`, `/proc/device-tree/thermal-zones` where accessible, and each `/sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone*/{type,temp,mode,policy,trip_point_*_temp,trip_point_*_type,trip_point_*_hyst}` where present. Match **type**, not zone number. | Confirms the three readings and actual kernel-exposed trips. Absence of an attribute is not proof of disabled hardware protection. Do not write `mode`, `policy`, `emul_temp`, or trips. |
| 2 | In the **same brief snapshot**, read `/sys/class/thermal/cooling_device*/{type,cur_state,max_state}`, `/sys/class/hwmon/*/{name,pwm1,fan1_input}` where present, `/sys/devices/system/cpu/cpufreq/policy*/{scaling_governor,scaling_cur_freq,scaling_max_freq}` where present, `/proc/pressure/cpu`, `/proc/loadavg`, and bounded process CPU use. | Shows exposed cooling state and CPU frequencies versus load/pressure at that moment. Frequency and cooling state do not reveal total board power or prove cooling effectiveness. |
| 3 | If temperatures are already high or rise quickly, **stop the probe and power off**; do not run stress tests, alter thermal controls, defeat throttling, or prolong the session to collect a baseline. If readings are safe enough for the owner to continue, a second short timestamped temperature/cooling snapshot can separate a rising transient from a falling post-boot transient. | An observed trend is more useful than a single number, but still cannot establish case temperature or normal idle range. |

Compare the **running device's** trips and DTB/kernel configuration before making a statement about the active thermal policy. The sysfs trip list and cooling state may not capture firmware-only protection.

## Gaps and confidence

- **High confidence:** Linux sysfs units, the limited meaning of PSI, and the product kernel's missing Linux fan/PWM drivers as configured at image commit `c36c76f3`. **Moderate confidence:** that the source's 90°C/95°C passive trips survived packaging unchanged; no local patch names them. **Unknown:** running DTB and firmware policy, sensor calibration, actual fan rotation, active cooling state, ongoing CPU/GPU use, battery temperature, and expected idle range. The source's 110°C critical trip is not a safety allowance.
- **Next offline source check:** identify and inspect the *exact packaged* Mini V2 Linux DTB from the deployed image if available. Compare its thermal zones, sensor references, trips, and cooling maps with the pinned source. The powered-off unit requires no action for this check.
